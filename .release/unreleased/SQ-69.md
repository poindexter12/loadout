---
ref: SQ-69
title: Say why the claim sweep left a claim alone, and let the control plane reclaim one under its own name
bump: minor
plugins: [sidequest]
---

`sweepClaims` now returns `skipped[]`, one entry per held claim it left alone, naming the holder, its measured board-quiet time, and which of the two thresholds actually governed the decision. This was the missing half of a confusing refusal: `claimReleaseVerdict` does not compare a single clock. A claim carrying a dispatch or an open verification marker is governed by the abandon backstop (24h by default), and the shorter idle threshold (1h by default) that `list` and the sweep result both advertise only ever governs a claim with no executor dispatch at all. A four-hour-idle dispatched claim therefore looked four times overdue while sitting at a sixth of the backstop that actually applied, and the sweep skipped it in silence. The result now reports `thresholds.idleMs` and `thresholds.abandonMs` together rather than one as though it governed the board.

`release` gained a real `force`. The flag and `skills/groom/SKILL.md` have long described forcing as overriding a live claim, but `opts.force` only ever reached the "already done" guard and never the ownership check, so an orchestrator releasing a dead executor's claim under its own identity got `not_owner` and the only thing that worked was passing the dead executor's id. A forced release now requires a recorded reason, bypasses the ownership check, and stamps `claimRelease.kind: "forced"` with `takenFrom` naming the dispossessed holder, so a control-plane reclaim is no longer indistinguishable in the record from a live agent handing back its own work. The MCP form takes over the claim only; it does not also drop a parked candidate or bypass the done guard the way CLI `--force` does.

Twenty-one tools that deliberately carry no description were suppressed with an empty string, which still serialized as `"description":""` in every `tools/list` payload. They are now suppressed with `undefined`, which `JSON.stringify` drops. That paid for the new `force` property and for stating its contract on `release`, and left the payload 127 bytes smaller than before rather than larger.

Refusals stop teaching impersonation. `not_owner` carried no message at all; it now explains why the board considers the claim live and names the forced path. The `groomClose` and hand-delivery `active_dispatch` refusals printed `sidequest release <ref> --by <claim holder>` verbatim, instructing the caller to act under the holder's identity; they now name the forced control-plane release instead.

---
ref: SQ-364
title: Dispatch payload byte caps measured the machine's temp path, not the payload
bump: patch
plugins: [sidequest]
---

Two payload ceilings failed the v4.14.0 cut on `main` and nothing about the payload was wrong. `test/mcp.test.ts` reported a warning dispatch at 1266 bytes against a 1220 cap, and `test/context-budget-benchmark.test.ts` reported a dispatch at 1370 against 1320. CI passed the identical commit, and so did `npm run test:files` run by hand on the machine that had just failed the cut. A green isolated run and a red full-suite run on one tree is not flakiness, and the obvious reading — that SQ-330 and SQ-324 had grown the payload past its budget — was wrong.

A compact dispatch payload embeds temp-directory paths several times over: the project path, the planned token file under `SIDEQUEST_HOME`, the worktree root. `Buffer.byteLength` of that payload therefore measured the length of the running machine's temp directory as much as the payload's own content, and the spread between environments is larger than the headroom these caps leave. Linux CI gets `/tmp`. macOS resolves `os.tmpdir()` to `/private/var/folders/<2>/<30>/T`, 56 characters. On top of that, `scripts/test-full.mjs` nests a 28-character suite sandbox under `TMPDIR`, which the file-scoped runner does not — the entire difference between the passing and failing commands.

Payload bytes rise with the length of those paths, and the rise is enough to carry both assertions over their ceilings. The relationship is not a clean multiple of the temp root, though: `scripts/test-full.mjs` also overrides `HOME`, `SIDEQUEST_HOME`, and `SIDEQUEST_CLAUDE_HOME`, so several independent path lengths move together and no single per-character coefficient holds across environments. What is measured on the failing base is the direction and the threshold. A control at the full-suite path length of 84 fails both assertions on `ca521ac8`, at 1239 against the 1220 cap and 1394 against 1320, and passes both at the same length once the measurement is normalized. A fixed-commit sweep over temp roots of 20, 49, 68, and 96 characters puts the crossover between them: the base passes at the two shorter lengths and fails at the two longer ones. Only the warning assertion fired in the cut because that payload sits closest to its ceiling, which is what made the failure look like it was about declared scope.

So the caps were never stale and the payload never grew: a cap of this shape cannot be honored by a measurement that moves with the checkout's temp path, and raising it would only move the platform it happens to fit. Every byte-cap assertion in both tests now normalizes each temp root to the Linux `/tmp` shape before measuring, longest root first so that `/private/var/folders/...` is not shortened into `/private/tmp` by its own unresolved prefix, and then asserts that no temp root survives the substitution — a payload that starts embedding a path in some new form fails loudly rather than quietly re-importing the drift. The ceilings themselves are unchanged at 1220 and 1320, which is the point: they now bound payload content, a new field or a longer prose string, which is the only thing they were ever able to police.

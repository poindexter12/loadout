---
ref: SQ-333
title: The executor briefing leads with its verification gate instead of burying it
bump: patch
plugins: [sidequest]
---

An executor decides how it will verify early, acts on whatever it has read by then, and has already paid for that decision by the time a later line of the briefing lands. The pinned verifier sat mid-way down the safety section, below the claim call, the worktree identity and the isolation contract, so an Opus dispatch on SQ-32 ran two full suites, over an hour of them with one killed by its own phase budget at load 28.85, ahead of an eight-second pinned gate that was the only run `submit` would accept. Other dispatches the same night did the same thing on a smaller scale. The information was never missing from the briefing; its placement was the defect.

The pinned verifier now opens the briefing as its own section, ahead of the claim call and the worktree contract, and it says what an executor planning verification needs in order to plan it correctly: this wrapper run is the gate rather than one option among several, it is the only run that produces the capture record `submit` requires, a passing full suite neither substitutes for it nor satisfies the gate, and a broad suite run alongside it is wasted work rather than extra rigour. The SQ-32 measurement is quoted in the section, because eight seconds against an hour is what makes the choice obvious.

The same section now tells an executor to take its verdict from the wrapper's recorded capture rather than from its own scan of runner output. The SQ-32 executor's hand-rolled summary grepped for TAP `not ok` while the runner printed a different failure marker, so a genuine red reported exit 0 and was caught only by reading the log directly. A wrapper that greps the wrong marker is a silent false-green, which is strictly worse than a lost verdict.

Nothing was duplicated to get the prominence: the verifier line, the wrapper invocation, the amendment notice, the evidence directory and the high-stakes verification block moved out of the safety section rather than being copied into a second one, and the safety heading no longer claims to cover verification.

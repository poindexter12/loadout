---
ref: SQ-324
title: Settle a dispatch's worktree write lease at the claim, and refuse loudly when it cannot be
bump: minor
plugins:
  - sidequest
---

The claim now reconciles a worktree-isolated dispatch's write lease against the checkout its executor
is actually standing in. A lease that creation attributed to a sibling is moved onto the claimant's
own checkout, taking the sibling's lease with it, and a claim made from inside the leased checkout
records that the lease is occupied so nothing can take it afterwards. A lease that cannot be
reconciled refuses the claim, naming the leased path, the path the executor is in, the agent that
checkout belongs to, the sibling ticket holding it, and the one legal next action; the refusal
authorizes that claimant to release with kind `technical_blocker`, the way an unbound runtime already
could.

Worktree creation cannot know which reservation a new checkout belongs to: its hook carries the
session and the path, so under a fan-out it attributes checkouts in board order. Seven isolated
executors spawned in one batch left two of them holding a lease on a sibling's checkout, and the
claim bound that guess as authoritative without ever consulting the agent's own cwd. Each of those
two could write nowhere: the worktree guard refused the leased path, the lease guard refused the one
they were in, and neither refusal could name the ticket whose lease was wrong, so both attempts
burned a full dispatch discovering it from a contradictory pair of `Edit` denials. The existing
creation-order repair could not fire, because it required both siblings to still be unclaimed and
identity-unbound, and on the recorded incident the sibling had claimed ten seconds before the crossed
reservation bound at all.

A single dispatch can land with no usable lease too, by reaching the board from the shared checkout or
with no worktree binding recorded. Both now refuse the claim with their own reason rather than
presenting as a write failure later.

The claim is where this belongs because it is the one call an executor makes holding a per-dispatch
token: the ticket-to-checkout pairing it reports is the only one in the system that is not a guess. An
observation under a token outranks creation order, and never outranks another runtime's proven
occupancy.

A terminal attempt whose claim was refused that way also stays retryable. Worktree recovery reads the
retained checkout's HEAD and refuses a retry when it carries an unintegrated candidate; under a
crossed lease that commit is the sibling's, which left the affected ticket with no legal path forward
at all and cost a replacement ticket to escape. A recorded claim refusal is proof the attempt never
claimed under that lease, so the retry proceeds and creates its own checkout. The sibling's checkout
is never resumed into and never removed.
